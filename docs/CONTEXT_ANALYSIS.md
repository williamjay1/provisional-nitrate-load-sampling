## Definitions and denominators

**Reference support.** A complete hour has all four joint-qualified original observations at minute 00, 15, 30, and 45. The existing A approval, nonestimated, noncensored, finite nonnegative, and UV-method conditions remain unchanged. Loads are the sum of four Q×C×900 s products, after unit conversion. No missing interval is filled or scaled to represent unobserved transport.

**Temporal concentration of transport.** For each station-year, the top-10% fraction is the load in the largest `ceil(0.10 × N)` complete-hour loads divided by load in all `N` complete hours. Ties do not enlarge that count. This is a post hoc concentration-of-transport description. The high-Q fraction uses complete reference hours whose original exact-hour Q is at least the 90th percentile of qualified Q in the preceding three calendar years, divided by all complete-hour reference load. Classification uses Q at the start of the hour, not the mean or maximum of future Q within it. The threshold is historical; its target-year load fraction is retrospective.

**Reference water coverage.** The main numerator sums Q×900 only on complete joint-qualified hours. Its primary denominator sums Q×900 over every available finite nonnegative quarter-grid Q reading, including estimated and provisional Q. A second ratio uses only individually quality-qualified Q in the denominator. Both denominators are independent of NOx availability. A third diagnostic uses all joint-qualified intervals, including those in incomplete hours, as the numerator. This distinguishes incomplete-hour exclusion from missing concentration support. The independent native sums match `hourly q_cms × q_count × 900` for every station-year, with numerical tolerance 1e-12 relative and 0.001 m³ absolute. These ratios concern **observed Q volume**, not unobserved total runoff.

**Early event transport.** For an event `[start, end)`, the first-24 h or first-72 h fraction sums complete-hour reference load at hour starts before `start + delay`, truncated at `end`, and divides by the complete-hour reference load of the event. This shows how much observed transport has occurred by a hypothetical return time if a sample were collected at the event onset. It is neither the actual policy's first usable arrival, nor an unavoidable estimation-error bound. Earlier samples and useful historical predictions can support estimates during this interval. First-window reference coverage is supplied next to each fraction. The file retains excluded events for coverage inspection, but load-timing figures should select `scorable=True`.

**Retained versus excluded events.** The original event definition and eligibility rules are unchanged. The event begins at its first Q≥historical Q90 hour and ends one hour after its last such hour; up to 24 intervening lower-Q hours can be bridged, but any missing Q breaks a sequence. Event span must be at least 12 h. Retention requires at least 95% complete reference hours, finite Q during the preceding and following 24 h, and positive observed reference load for relative-error scoring. Tables retain coverage failure and boundary uncertainty separately. Comparisons include peak Q, duration, start season, complete reference at the Q peak, gap length, gap counts by temporal thirds, and missing hours before versus after the peak. “Edge” means the first or last 24 h; those windows overlap in events shorter than 48 h. Excluded events with no missing concentration support can still have uncertain Q boundaries.

## Historical residual persistence and target diagnostics

The cached `history_base` is fitted to the full history and therefore **is not used** as a forward residual estimate. For every included station-year, the analysis reproduces the original seed-41 64-tree RF calibrated on the latest historical year with at least 1,000 qualified exact-hour Q/NOx labels. Training uses only earlier years within the original three-year history, with the original deterministic 12,000-label cap. The resulting model predicts every hour of that calibration year; only qualified exact-hour pairs supply residuals. The original audit, which used up to 4,000 calibration labels, is then reproduced from the same sampled indices. Bias and noise MSE matched in all 43 cases. All training timestamps precede the calibration year, and all calibration timestamps precede the target year.

The primary autocorrelation at 6, 24, 72, and 168 h requires exact timestamp separation **and every intervening hour and both endpoints to be jointly qualified**. `pairs` reports the actual denominator. A secondary endpoint-only analysis describes sensitivity to the stricter uninterrupted-support requirement. Adjacent rows after dropping missing values are never treated as adjacent hours. Persistence is Pearson residual correlation over the historical calibration year; long seasonal structure and uneven qualified support remain possible contributors. This diagnostic does not identify an optimal memory constant, prove a mechanism, or imply that a chosen correction is safe at every event.

Target zero-label results use the original final historical predictor and all three model seeds. `context_zero_label_station_year.csv` averages seeds within station-year; it retains missing event endpoints for station-years without eligible events. For a paper-level event summary, average eligible years within station and then stations. Do not pool all events or treat seeds as replicate rivers.

`context_forward_model.csv` includes historical forward residual bias/RMSE/MAE, target zero-label residual bias/RMSE/MAE, concentration means, and target-minus-history differences. Historical and target models are fitted to different permitted training records. Thus the target-minus-history difference is an **apparent forward-transfer discrepancy**, with model refitting, observed-support selection, and environmental changes entangled. It is not an isolated estimate of concept drift and is not known before target monitoring. No complex correlation or regression model was fitted to the nine event sites.

## Independent unit example

The archived JSON explicitly reports `00060: ft3/s` and `99133: mg/l as N`. Independently deriving the factor from the exact international foot gives `1 ft³/s = 0.3048³ m³/s = 0.028316846592 m³/s`. For a 15-minute interval:

`kg N = Q(ft³/s) × 0.3048³ × 900 s × C(mg N/L) × 1000 L/m³ × 10⁻⁶ kg/mg`.

At station 02035000 on 2025-01-01 00:00 UTC, raw Q=7350 ft³/s and NOx=0.34 mg/L as N give Q=208.1288224512 m³/s, interval volume 187315.94020608 m³, and interval load 63.6874196700672 kg N. The next two quarters have the same recorded values; at 00:45 Q=7320 ft³/s. Summing all four raw quarters gives 748499.205966336 m³ and **254.489730028554 kg N**, matching the frozen hourly record. This checks absolute units independently of percentage-error cancellation. The full raw paths, hashes, unit objects and four timestamps are in `context_unit_audit.json`.

## Remaining evidence limits

Missing concentration transport is unidentified; these calculations do not create a reference for excluded intervals. Numeric-Q coverage includes retrospectively available readings and does not prove real-time service availability. The same UV sensor supplies virtual samples and reference concentrations, so independent chemical truth is not established. HUC4 groupings and nearby/upstream/downstream sites constrain independent environmental replication. Historical forward correlations and target diagnostics show observed patterns, not causal mechanisms or a deployment rule for unseen sites. All 2023–2025 outcomes were already viewed before this revision, and the new diagnostics retain that post-result identity.
