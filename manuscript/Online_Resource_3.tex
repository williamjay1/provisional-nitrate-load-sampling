\abstract{Earlier experiment settings, historical calendar designs, reconstruction sensitivities and diagnostic provenance for the sparse river nutrient monitoring assessment.}



\keywords{experiment settings; calendar design; reconstruction sensitivity}



\maketitle



This resource documents the earlier experiment that produced the pooled residual results, together with the sensitivity analyses that followed it. The comparisons reported in the main text were specified after those results had been inspected, so the material here keeps both stages of evidence readable side by side and supports the analysis status described in the Materials and methods. The earlier configuration and its comparison tables are reproduced below without alteration.

The historical QR and D optimal calendars are calendar adaptations rather than complete replications of published sparse sensing systems. Their rank is capped at eight while the smallest budget is 24 samples, so QR pivots after the basis rank can reflect numerical ordering, and both calendars allow unrestricted seasonal allocation unlike block quota policies. They are reported for completeness and they support no superiority claim against the published methods.

Innovation scale adaptation in the earlier zero rate comparison changes both subsequent query weighting and reconstruction weighting, so it does not isolate a query only mechanism. The 6 h and 72 h sensitivity changes actual information availability and the subsequent calendars, which differs from the fixed real delay score ablation of the main text. Tables F1 to F4 retain these distinctions.

\section*{S1. Predictor specification and historical calibration}

Random forests used 64 trees, a maximum depth of 12, a minimum leaf size of 16, bootstrap sampling and otherwise default regression settings. At most 12,000 qualified historical pairs were selected without replacement for each fit. Inputs were log scaled discharge together with its 1, 6, 24 and 72 h lags and differences, an exponentially weighted log discharge mean with span 24, a discharge missingness indicator, and annual sine and cosine terms. Discharge scaling used its qualified median over the permitted historical block, and forward filling stopped after 24 h, after which the historical median was used. The annual phase used day of year and a 365.25 day denominator.

For residual scale calibration, the latest historical year containing at least 1,000 qualified pairs supplied up to 4,000 calibration responses, and a forest fitted to preceding years predicted those responses. Discharge scaling used the complete permitted historical block including that calibration year, which makes this chronological response withholding rather than a fully nested preprocessing experiment. No target year responses entered either the calibration or the final historical fit. The initial coefficient variance and the measurement error proxy equalled the forward mean squared error, with the numerical floor specified in the main text.

The nine component residual representation consisted of an intercept and four cosine and sine pairs. For standardised context $\widetilde x$, each pair was $\cos(\omega_j^\mathsf T\widetilde x)/2$ and $\sin(\omega_j^\mathsf T\widetilde x)/2$ with $\omega_j\sim N(0,I_4)$, and context standard deviations were bounded below at $10^{-6}$. Frequencies, standardisation and the 96 context reference library were fixed before each replay, and library candidates required observed, finite and nonnegative discharge, so filled values created no candidate observations. One seed generated each station year shared forest, feature map and reference library, and noise adaptation was bounded between 0.1 and 10 times its initial value.

\section*{S2. Historical calendar designs}

Each permitted historical year contributed separate daily discharge and concentration columns to a 366 position month and day matrix, and qualified observations were averaged within each day. Columns required at least 180 observed days, and gaps were linearly interpolated with endpoint filling solely to construct the historical design matrix. Each column was centred and divided by its sample standard deviation, effectively constant columns were discarded, and neither missing reference loads nor target concentrations were ever filled by this operation.

A thin singular value decomposition retained the smallest rank that explained at least 85\% of the squared singular value energy, capped at eight. Historical QR applied a column pivoted QR decomposition to $U_rU_r^\mathsf T$. Historical D optimal initialised $M=10^{-6}I$, repeatedly selected the unchosen calendar row that maximised $u_j^\mathsf TM^{-1}u_j$ and added $u_ju_j^\mathsf T$ to $M$, with ties following calendar order. The highest ranked dates present in the target calendar were sampled at 12:00 UTC and 29 February was skipped in nonleap years, and if no historical columns qualified the fallback ranking was chronological.

These calendar adaptations share the historical basis rationale of sparse sensing but not an entire published reconstruction pipeline. QR selection beyond the basis rank can depend on numerical pivot ordering, and the ridge and greedy oversampling of the D optimal calendar are explicit implementation choices. Both calendars permit unrestricted seasonal allocation under the annual budget and minimum spacing, unlike the five quota policies, and no target year completeness mask was used to avoid failed samples.

\section*{S3. Fixed experiment factors}

The development grid was C0 with a 72 h look ahead window, a 720 h half life and a 0.85 threshold quantile; C1 with 168 h, 720 h and 0.85; C2 with 72 h, 2160 h and 0.85; and C3 with 72 h, 720 h and 0.65. Selection used seed 41, all three budgets and the five quota policies, averaging available years within station and weighting stations, policies and budgets equally, and C2 was selected as the common configuration.

The temporal and spatial assessments crossed budgets of 24, 48 and 96 samples with seeds 41, 73 and 109, seven samplers and three reconstruction modes, at a delay of 24 h and a noise adaptation rate of 0.1. The unchanged forest supplied a separate comparator with no new labels, and uniform phases were 0.25, 0.50 and 0.75. All samplers respected a 6 h grid and a 24 h minimum spacing. Prespecified temporal sensitivities changed the delay to 6 or 72 h or set the adaptation rate to zero for the uniform, flow, uncertainty and sampling value policies with online reconstruction, and the zero adaptation comparison changes both future query weighting and residual measurement weighting rather than isolating a query only intervention. These factors were recorded before the policy outcomes were inspected.

\section*{S4. Reconstruction sensitivities on fixed calendars}

Two additional online reconstructors were evaluated on calendars that had already been purchased. Let $s$ index the latest successful sample returned by time $t$, with arrival time $a_s$. The first estimated

\begin{equation}
\widehat c_t=\max\{0,c_0(x_t)+[c_s-c_0(x_s)] \exp[-(t-a_s)/168]\}.
\end{equation}

The 168 h value is an exponential time constant measured from arrival and inherited from the earlier pilot, and it is not a 168 h half life. The second reconstructor held the latest returned concentration constant until another response arrived. Both used the unchanged forest before the first return and excluded responses arriving beyond the reporting horizon.

This was a descriptive check of reconstruction dependence with no new parameter search. The sampling value and uncertainty calendars had originally been generated from the Bayesian residual state and were not regenerated under the replacement predictors, so these results compare alternative reconstruction of fixed measurements rather than the performance of newly evaluated closed loop policies.

\section*{S5. Diagnostic definitions and provenance}

Event capture means at least one attempt within the event. Timely return within the event requires a successful sample collected during the event and returned strictly before its end, and any return additionally includes samples collected before event onset but returned during the event. The denominator for all three proportions is all defined events rather than only captured events, and proportions are averaged over seeds within station before equal station averaging. Stations with no defined events have undefined proportions and are excluded from these diagnostic means, so the 2025 all event means use 12 stations and the eligible event means use nine, where the latter apply the reference coverage and boundary rules before aggregation. Receiving a label does not by itself establish that prediction improves.

Fallback frequency is deadline only attempts divided by annual attempts. Exact calendar overlap is the intersection divided by the union of timestamps, and the 6 h overlap matches timestamps one to one and reports twice the matched count divided by total attempts across the pair. These diagnostics include all 13 data eligible stations, and capture and error associations retain fixed capture states without estimating causal effects.
